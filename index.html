11Jesus continued: “There was a man who had two sons.
12The younger one said to his father, ‘Father, give me my share of the estate.’ So he divided his property between them.
13Not long after that, the younger son gathered all he had and set off for a distant country.
14After he had spent everything, there was a severe famine in that country, and he began to be in need.
15So he went and hired himself out to a citizen of that country, who sent him to his fields to feed pigs.
16He longed to fill his stomach with the pods that the pigs were eating, but no one gave him anything.
17When he came to his senses, he said, ‘How many of my father’s hired servants have food to spare, and here I am starving to death!’
18I will set out and go back to my father and say to him: Father, I have sinned against heaven and against you.
19I am no longer worthy to be called your son; make me like one of your hired servants.
20But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion for him; he ran to his son, embraced him and kissed him.
21The son said to him, ‘Father, I have sinned against heaven and against you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son.’
22But the father said to his servants, ‘Quick! Bring the best robe and put it on him. Put a ring on his finger and sandals on his feet.’
23Bring the fattened calf and kill it. Let’s have a feast and celebrate.
24For this son of mine was dead and is alive again; he was lost and is found.
25Meanwhile, the older son was in the field.
28The older brother became angry and refused to go in. So his father went out and pleaded with him.
32But we had to celebrate and be glad, because this brother of yours was dead and is alive again; he was lost and is found.

Word Study — Luke 15:20

ἐσπλαγχνίσθη
Transliteration
esplanchnisthē
Sense in context
To be moved with deep compassion or pity.
Why it matters
The verse emphasizes the father’s inward compassion before his outward actions of running, embracing, and kissing.
δραμών
Transliteration
dramōn
Sense in context
Having run / running.
Why it matters
The father does not wait passively for the son to finish the journey home.

⚠️ DON'T OVERREAD IT

Original-language study should clarify the text, not manufacture secret meanings. Published versions should cite verified lexical sources.

The World Behind the Word

🏺 HONOR & SHAME

Jesus’ story takes place in a culture where family honor, public reputation, and household loyalty mattered deeply. The younger son’s earlier actions would have brought serious shame to the family.

Strong evidence

🏃 THE FATHER RUNS

The image of a household patriarch running publicly would have been striking and intentionally undignified by elite social expectations. It heightens the father’s willingness to move toward his disgraced son.

Probable / culturally grounded

🪨 WAS THE SON ABOUT TO BE STONED?

Luke does not say villagers were preparing to stone the returning son. That claim appears in some later interpretations and should not be presented as a fact of the passage.

Debated interpretation

👘 ROBE, RING & SANDALS

The father’s gifts publicly communicate welcome and restored standing in the household rather than treating the son merely as a hired worker.

Probable

What You Might Have Missed

01 — THE INHERITANCE

The younger son asks for his share while his father is still alive, creating a profound breach in the relationship.

02 — THE DISTANT COUNTRY

Leaving family, land, and household intensifies the son's separation.

03 — THE PIGS

For a Jewish audience, working among pigs underscores the depth of the son's degradation and desperation.

04 — “SINNED AGAINST HEAVEN”

The son's confession recognizes both offense before God and offense against his father.

05 — THE FATHER SEES HIM

The story emphasizes the father's attention to his returning son before the son reaches the house.

06 — THE FATHER RUNS

The father willingly abandons expected dignity in order to move toward his son.

07 — ROBE, RING & SANDALS

The father publicly restores the son's place in the household.

08 — THE OLDER BROTHER

The story does not end with the younger brother. The older brother also resists the father and refuses to enter the celebration.
